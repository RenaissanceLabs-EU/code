Training starts with one sentence: I want a model that does X, and I will know it works when Y improves. Everything else, the data, the method and the compute, follows from X and Y. This page helps you write that sentence, then pick the approach that fits it, your data and your budget.

Each entry is tagged with how settled the practice is. Established means it is widely used and has been stable for years. Emerging means it appears in recent open recipes but is still changing. Experimental means there is little independent evidence yet.

1. Pick the capability

"Good at code" is several different skills. They need different signals, different data and different tests, so the choice matters more than any hyperparameter.

2. Pick the approach

Five approaches cover almost every project. They differ in what they change in the model, how much data they need and how much computing they cost. The figures in the third column are examples from published work, not rules.

ApproachWhat it changesScale in published examplesChoose it whenMain risk
Pretraining from scratch EstablishedEverything. The model learns code, language and general knowledge from nothing.StarCoder2 was trained on 3.3 to 4.3 trillion tokens, counting repeated passes over a smaller dataset ({{r:paper-starcoder2}}).You need a new tokenizer, a new architecture, a clean-room license or full control of the data, and you have a large budget.Cost. A mistake found late is expensive.
Continued pretraining EstablishedWhat the model knows about a domain, such as a language, a library or a codebase.Code Llama continued from Llama 2 with 500 billion to 1 trillion tokens; the model recipes show other cases.The base model is strong but weak in your language or domain, and you have many tokens of it.Forgetting general ability. Mix general data back in.
Supervised fine-tuning EstablishedHow the model responds: format, style, which task it attempts.From about a thousand carefully chosen examples ({{r:paper-lima}}, a general instruction-tuning study) to tens of thousands (the OSS-Instruct set has about 75,000 rows) and millions (OpenCodeInstruct has 5 million).The model has the knowledge but does not follow your instructions or format. The cheapest first step.Teaching the style of the teacher, errors included.
Preference tuning Established for DPOWhich of two answers the model favors.Thousands to hundreds of thousands of pairs (see {{r:acecodepair-300k}}).SFT has plateaued and you can build pairs from test results.Rewarding length or style when pairs come from a judge model.
Execution-based reinforcement learning EmergingWhether the model's code actually works, learned by trying.DeepCoder-14B trained on about 24,000 problems; see {{r:recipe-deepcoder}}.Your model already solves some tasks, you have a trustworthy verifier, and you can afford many rollouts.Reward hacking when the tests are weak or reachable.

4. Write the sentence down

Before you collect anything, fill in four blanks. If you cannot, the project is not yet defined.

  • Capability: one row of the first table.
  • Measurement: one benchmark or private test set that fits it, and the number you start from.
  • Data: where the examples come from and what license each carries.
  • Budget: the largest run you are willing to pay for, and the smaller run that tells you whether to start it.

The next step is choosing the data that gives the signal your capability needs.
